By Alyssa Schnugg

Springfield Family Life Center, the nonprofit arm of Springfield Missionary Baptist Church in Abbeville, received nearly $6,000 in grants during a recent Night for Nonprofits event.

The center received a grant from the Lafayette Oxford Foundation for Tomorrow, was named the event’s Crowd Favorite and received an additional LOFT grant awarded to organizations that lost food and other resources because of the storm.

Darlene Malone, first lady of Springfield Missionary Baptist Church, said the grants will help the center continue its food pantry and other programs while allowing it to expand its services.

The Springfield Family Life Center has operated a food pantry since 2023. It is open from 10 a.m. to noon on the first and third Thursday of each month. The pantry is available to anyone in need, not just church members, Malone said.

The center also operates the Prime Timers program for adults 55 and older. Originally called Meals on Wheels, the program provides hot meals to seniors on Tuesdays, Wednesdays and Thursdays at the Life Center. Participants also take part in activities such as bingo and receive information on topics including how to avoid scams.

The center also delivers food to seniors and others who cannot travel to the pantry.

Malone said the center’s work during the ice storm brought additional attention to its mission. The organization partnered with other groups to provide food, water, hygiene products and blankets to residents who were without electricity and unable to cook.

The center provided a location for Chick-fil-A to distribute free food and partnered with Forged by Fire, a Christian organization that brought a food truck to the church property to prepare meals. The center also worked with other organizations to serve hot meals and distribute water and other supplies.

Malone said the center continues to respond to emergency needs outside its regular pantry hours. People who need food can contact the center, and volunteers will work to provide assistance.

The grants will primarily support existing programs. Malone said the center hopes to expand programs for young people, including efforts to help students prepare for college. The center has previously offered ACT workshops.

Malone’s husband, the Rev. Jerry Malone, is pastor of Springfield Missionary Baptist Church. He recently celebrated his 20th anniversary as pastor of the church.

For Malone, the center’s work is an extension of the church’s Christian mission.

“If we’re Christians, we’re supposed to be Christ-like,” she said. “We’re God’s hands and feet here on Earth.”

She said that means helping people regardless of their circumstances or where they live.

“Love is an action word,” Malone said. “You have to be about it.”
